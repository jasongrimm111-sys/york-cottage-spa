The big rules

What the booking system always makes sure of, on the website and in the admin.

  • A day or session is only offered if every guest’s included treatment can be given a time, after the lunch or food service has been staffed.
  • An extra treatment is only offered if everyone’s treatments still fit with it.
  • Guests choose a time for every treatment before they can pay. Changing the date or session clears their treatment choices and times.
  • Just before paying, the times are checked again and held for 30 minutes. If two people pay for the same time at once, only the first gets it; the other is asked to choose again and isn’t charged.
  • Treatments are planned at the earliest free time. Guests are put side by side only if that delays them by 30 minutes or less.
  • Treatments can start on the hour and half hour, or back to back, as soon as a therapist and room are free again (to the nearest 5 minutes).
  • Treatments only can still be booked while the spa is booked “just for” a group, but never during the hours a whole-house stay has the spa.
  • On a whole-house check-in day, morning sessions and treatments only can still be booked (before the house has the spa); on its check-out day, afternoon and evening sessions and treatments only can. Days in between are the house’s alone. Groups can’t be on any day of a whole-house stay.
  • No therapist is booked over their hours, breaks, time off, food duty, or daily hands-on limit.

How treatments are planned

  • Every treatment is followed by at least 15 minutes to reset the room (longer if the treatment says so).
  • Treatments-only guests are asked to arrive 15 minutes before their first treatment. Change
  • When more than one therapist is working: fill one therapist’s day before using the next, in this order: Gemma Burke, Georgie Parker. Cover therapists are only booked when the others are full.
  • Groups of 2 or more are treated side by side where it costs at most 30 minutes.
  • A therapist does at most 6 hours of hands-on treatments a day (unless their own limit is set in Staff).
  • Treatment rooms: Treatment room 1 (2 beds), Treatment room 2 (1 bed).
  • A therapist only ever treats one guest at a time, and a treatment room is one guest’s at a time.
  • Two guests from the same booking can be treated together in a 2-bed room (each with their own therapist, at the same time) when they ask for it while booking. Guests from different bookings never share a room.

Staff cover for busy days

How the system spots days that need another therapist, and how to add one just for that day.

  • How treatments are shared: each therapist’s day is filled before the next is used, so a busy day shows clearly when one person is nearly full. Change
  • Staffing suggestions look six weeks ahead and flag a day when: therapist time is 85%+ booked; a therapist is within 90% of their daily hands-on limit; a session still has places but no therapist time is left for its included treatments (so it can’t be sold online); only the included treatments fit, so guests can’t add extras; or no therapist is rostered while the spa is open.
  • Red means sales are being lost or someone is over their limit; amber means worth considering. They show on the Rota, in the To do list, and at the top of each day on Future days.
  • To add cover: press Add cover on a suggestion (or on the day). Pick a therapist who isn’t working that day, or add someone new, and set their hours. It’s a one-off shift for that day only; their usual week doesn’t change.
  • When cover is added, the day’s booked treatments are shared out between everyone on shift where possible. Guests keep exactly the same times; only who treats them (and which room) can change, and you see the list before anything moves. Therapists whose treatments change are emailed their new list (if they have schedule alerts on).
  • As soon as cover is on the rota, the website can sell more that day: more times for extras and longer treatments, and sessions that had no therapist time become bookable again.
  • Cover-only therapists (Staff → Edit → “Cover only”) are only booked when the regular therapists are full, whichever sharing rule is chosen. Nobody is marked cover-only yet; anyone new added through Add cover is. On days they aren’t rostered they add nothing to what the website offers.

Therapists’ lunch and breaks

The planner leaves room for these in everyone’s day.

  • No one works more than 4 hours without a break of 15–30 minutes.
  • On days over 6 hours, a lunch of 30–60 minutes between 11:30 and 15:00.
  • Food duty counts as work, so a therapist serving lunch still needs their break around it.

How the prices work

  • Half days are 4 hours: Spa Access Only £50 and Spa, Lunch & Treatment £100 per person.
  • Full days (7½ hours) cost less per hour than two half days. Spa Access Only full day £75 (half day + 50%). Spa, Lunch & Treatment full day £135: the same spa time, but lunch and the treatment are only had once, so it adds £35 to the half day rather than doubling it. Both fill the spa from 09:30 to 17:00, so a full-day guest takes the place of a morning and an afternoon guest; the premium covers most of the second half day, and a longer day is a reason to book.
  • The difference between the two spa days is lunch and a treatment: £50 per person for a half day.
  • Spa Break: £150 for the first night, then £100 for each extra night. Each night includes the room, spa time and lunch. Extra nights cost less than the first because check-in, changeover and the room clean happen once per stay.
  • Room to themselves +£50 a night: break prices are based on 2 sharing, so a guest alone in a room pays towards the empty half. Not charged when the break has 6 or more guests or is booked “just for us”: those nights are closed to other guests, so every bedroom is theirs and a guest sleeping alone takes no room that could have been sold.
  • Whole House: £1,840 a night. It’s the only booking that gives a group the whole house, and it must include Friday and Saturday, when hen parties and big groups want it.
  • Head spa is 10% off with a spa day or stay.
  • Group discount: 10% off for 6 or more guests on Spa Access Only, Spa, Lunch & Treatment and Spa Breaks, to help more groups book. A group of 6 fills the spa in one sale (no empty places to sell and less admin), so a small discount is worth it, and they also get the spa to themselves. It’s off the booking price only: extra treatments, where most groups spend more, and breakfast stay full price. Not on the Whole House, which is already priced for the group. Only one discount per booking, so a code or the head spa offer is used instead if it saves more. Example: 6 on a half-day Spa, Lunch & Treatment pay £540 instead of £600.

“Just for us” (exclusive use)

  • 6 or more guests have it free. The spa holds 6 at once, so a booking of 6 fills it anyway. On a spa break, 6+ also means no other guests stay those nights.
  • Fewer than 6 pay for each empty place, at about half the price of that place: £25 per empty place for a half day and £40 for a full day (half of Spa Access Only, because what the business loses is spa space, not lunch or treatments), and £75 per empty place per night on a spa break (half the first-night price, because the spare bedrooms close too).
  • Why half price: empty places wouldn’t all have sold, so charging about half of each one leaves the business level on average, while a couple pays far less than for 6 people. Exclusive bookings also tend to add extra treatments.
  • The fewer guests, the more it costs, but the more of them, the better value it is. The guest sees the exact amount on the tick box, on the half-day / full-day buttons and as its own line in the summary before paying.
  • Last minute: 50% off when booked 7 days or fewer before the visit, because empty places that late rarely sell.
  • Only offered when nobody else is booked in. If a day can’t be just theirs, the guest is told and can choose another day or carry on without it.
